Design and preview

Does the preview show the finished board or the full rough blank?

It shows the finished board face. Rough machining overage is reported in the engineering guidance and material estimate, but it is not added around the visual preview.

Why does the Designer start with finished dimensions?

The entries are the requested finished size. The Designer works backward from them, then reports the Actual Board Dimensions produced by complete crosscuts, complete laminated rows, and entered borders. It never hides a row calculation or silently trims away a good laminated row.

Why can I change thickness without seeing a dramatically different pattern?

If the nearest whole crosscut count and laminated-row count remain the same, the arrangement can look similar. The thickness still changes the lamination requirement, rough-crosscut guidance, calculated finished run, master-blank length, material quantity, and the physical size represented by each crosscut.

Why are the diamonds kept square instead of stretched to fill the preview?

Stretching would show a shape that cannot be built from square end-grain faces. The Designer keeps one uniform scale, centers any length overage, and clips only at the finished ends.

Why does Starting Crosscut change the number of full diamonds I see?

It changes which piece receives the first 180-degree turn in the alternating glue-up. One choice can center a full diamond near an end, while the other can place partial diamonds at both ends and one fewer full diamond through the center. It is the same collection of crosscuts in a different physical orientation; dimensions and material quantities do not change.

Are the wood colors exact?

No. Colors help identify species and follow the pattern. Actual wood varies by tree, board, grain, age, sanding, lighting, and finish. Custom woods can be added with a name and approximate preview color.

What does “Required Lamination Size Before 45° Cuts” mean?

It is the practical square size of the laminated blank before the first 45-degree cuts. The mathematical minimum is finished thickness × √2. The Designer rounds up to the nearest 1/8 inch so the blank is not undersized.

Why must the strip total match the required pre-45° lamination size?

The entered strips form the physical laminated blank before its four 45-degree cuts. Their combined width must therefore equal the Required Lamination Size Before 45° Cuts, not the finished board thickness. For a 1.500-inch finished board, that rounded requirement is 2.125 inches. The Designer shows both values and warns until they match.

Crosscuts and kerf

How is the calculated crosscut count chosen?

The Designer divides finished length by finished thickness and uses the nearest whole crosscut count. That actual count drives the preview and manufacturing result.

Why does an odd crosscut count show a warning?

Alternating pieces form mirrored partners. An odd count leaves a different pattern termination at the two ends, so the finished board is visibly unbalanced. The Designer shows the real odd result instead of hiding it.

Why isn't there an Alternate Even Option?

An automatic even override could silently change the physical result. The current Designer keeps the requested finished dimensions as the source of truth. Change the finished length or thickness yourself when an even count is preferred.

What should I enter for blade kerf?

Enter the actual thickness of the blade making the crosscuts. Kerf is the material removed by each cut and directly increases the required master-blank length.

What is the recommended rough crosscut?

It is the finished thickness plus approximately 1/8 inch for flattening and cleanup. It is starting guidance, not a substitute for the allowance required by your process.

What does the required rough master-blank length include?

It includes every assigned rough crosscut plus the blade kerf between adjacent pieces. The result appears inside the single calculated crosscut result.

Top and bottom borders

Why do borders remove two laminated rows at a time?

The top and bottom borders are mirrored. Each border threshold therefore removes one complete row from the top and one complete row from the bottom. This prevents a design that would require ripping a patterned row lengthwise.

How does the finished-thickness border calculation work?

For a 12.875-inch board at 1.500-inch finished thickness, 1.3125 inches of entered border per edge leaves 10.250 inches available. Six complete 1.500-inch cells fit, creating a 9.000-inch diamond field. The completed border must therefore total 1.9375 inches per edge.

Why does the border panel say “still needed” or “too wide”?

Your entered physical bands are compared with the width needed to reach the requested overall board width at a complete mirrored row-pair boundary. You may adjust the bands until the check reads Matched ✓, accept the displayed Actual Board Dimensions, or change the requested board width.

Can I use several border colors?

Yes. Add as many bands as required and choose the width and species for each. The schedule is mirrored at both long edges.

What if all border bands use the same species?

Add one entry for every physical band even when adjacent bands use the same species. Four same-color bands require four entries; the Designer does not merge them.

Do borders increase the finished board width?

The actual width is the complete diamond field plus the entered border widths on both edges. If that total differs from the requested width, the Actual Board Dimensions card warns you. The Designer does not resize or trim those physical parts to force the requested number.

When are borders glued on?

Complete the long master blank first, then glue the borders to both long edges before crosscutting. This produces bordered end-grain pieces when the master blank is sliced.

Edge Rip

What does the Edge Rip control change?

It changes only the two 45-degree corner areas removed from the completed first-cut section. Replacement wood fills only those removed areas; the original laminate geometry does not stretch or change.

When is the Edge Rip performed?

Make all four initial 45-degree cuts first. Then cut across the designated center section, prepare matching 45-degree replacement pieces, and glue them to the fresh cut edges.

How do I make a design without an Edge Rip?

Select Zero. The replacement geometry and Edge Rip steps are then omitted from the preview guidance and printable procedure.

Materials, waste, and cost

Why is the wood cost an estimate?

Every shop and board is different. Defects, grain selection, milling, machine setup, available board sizes, and workflow can change the actual amount purchased and used.

What does estimated rough lumber include?

It counts every physical rough-ripped strip in every laminated row, the required pre-45° blank size, full kerf-inclusive master-blank length, borders on both edges, Edge Rip replacement wood, and the selected waste allowance. Identical laminate cuts are grouped for the printable total, and a matching same-species center pair is counted as one wider strip with one rough-rip allowance. The Designer displays the exact row-count × row-length basis used for the laminate board feet. Finished-board volume is not shown.

Why does waste default to 35% or 40%?

The recommended minimum is 35% when there is no Edge Rip and 40% when an Edge Rip is selected. You may enter another value, but a warning remains visible below the recommendation.

What is included in Estimated Wood Cost?

It is the estimated rough lumber for each species multiplied by the board-foot prices you enter. It does not include glue, sandpaper, finish, blades, electricity, labor, or other shop supplies.

Are border and Edge Rip woods counted and combined correctly?

Yes. Each physical border band is counted on both long edges. The full original laminate remains in the estimate because it must be built before Edge Rip; replacement wood is then added separately. Shared species are combined into one purchasing row.

Projects, exporting, and build guides

What does Save Project store?

The downloaded JSON file stores the design dimensions, strips, border bands, Edge Rip, woods, custom colors, prices, kerf, and waste choices. Use Open Project to continue later.

What is Export SVG for?

It downloads the current finished-board design as scalable vector artwork. Use the engineering values and printed plan for machining dimensions rather than measuring the on-screen image.

What is the difference between the Illustrated Build Guide and Sample Build?

The Illustrated Build Guide is generated from the active project, including its woods, counts, dimensions, and selected operations. The Sample Build is an independent real-photo reference showing one actual build.

Can I print the User Guide, FAQ, or Sample Build?

Yes. Each page has its own print control. Printing the FAQ opens every answer first. The Sample Build can also be printed independently without altering the active design.

Troubleshooting

The Sample Build photos do not appear. What should I do?

Use Designer v3.0.52 or newer. All 20 photos are embedded directly in the Sample Build page, so they no longer depend on a nested photo folder. If an older copy is open, close it and open the current version.

How do I enlarge a Sample Build photo?

Click the photo, or focus it with the keyboard and press Enter or Space. Use the on-screen arrows or keyboard left/right arrows to move between photos. Close with the × button, the background, or Escape.

Why does an old saved project look slightly different after opening?

The Designer migrates supported older data into current controls. Current previews use finished thickness for square-cell spacing; strip widths determine the bands inside each cell. This can correct the row and length proportions shown by an older release. Review warnings and save a new copy after confirming it.

What should I check before trusting a material total?

Confirm the Actual Board Dimensions, strips, Edge Rip, border widths, species, kerf, and waste; review any requested-size or border warnings; then compare the result with the actual boards available from your supplier.

Still unsure?Keep the finished-board preview, printed illustrated guide, and real-photo Sample Build together. Use the project's calculated dimensions and counts, and use the photos to confirm order and orientation.
