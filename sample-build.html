This guide does not change your current design

Use these real workshop photos when a generated diagram needs more context. Dimensions, woods, and quantities shown in your active project remain separate.

Photo guide in progressFour original build photos are included now. Clearly marked spaces remain for the earlier preparation and final finishing photos.
Workshop reminder: Confirm your own measurements and machine setup before every cut. Use guards, push devices, eye protection, hearing protection, and dust collection appropriate to the operation.
1

Mill the lumber

Mill the selected lumber square and straight, allowing extra material for planing and sanding.

Original workshop photo to be addedRough boards and milled stock
2

Rip and arrange the laminate strips

Rip the strips oversize, finish them consistently, and dry-fit the species in the planned order before glue is applied.

Original workshop photo to be addedNumbered strip order
3

Glue and flatten the laminated blank

Clamp the strip assembly evenly, then flatten and mill the blank square to the required size before the 45° cuts.

Original workshop photo to be addedBlank in clamps and squared blank
4

Complete the first four 45° cuts

Mark the centers of adjacent edges and make the four 45° cuts before beginning the Edge Rip operation.

Original workshop photo to be addedSquare blank and four cut sections
5

Original build photo

Cut the Edge Rip

After the first 45° cuts are finished, cut across the walnut section. The opposing angled shoulders are visible on the left and right sides of this end view.

6

Original build photo

Glue the new 45° maple onto the cut walnut edges

Fit the maple replacement pieces to the freshly cut walnut edges. Keep the mirrored assemblies aligned so the light lines meet cleanly when the pattern is assembled.

7

Original build photo

Assemble the long master blank and glue borders before crosscutting

Complete the long patterned blank first. When the design includes borders, glue them to the two long edges now—before any crosscuts are made.

8

Original build photo

Mark the crosscuts from the top view

View the completed master blank from above. Mark each crosscut perpendicular to the long strips using the spacing and kerf calculated for the active project.

9

Rotate and lay out the crosscuts

Keep the crosscuts in order. Rotate every second piece and dry-fit the diamond field before the final glue-up.

Original workshop photo to be addedCrosscuts arranged into diamonds
10

Glue, flatten, trim, sand, and finish

Glue the final field, flatten both faces, trim to size, sand through the selected grits, and apply the chosen finish.
